In plain English

Think of DeepSeek as the company that broke AI pricing. A small Chinese team (~150 people, funded by a hedge fund) released open-weight models that match GPT-4 quality at roughly 1/30th the cost. When they shipped R1 in January 2025, NVIDIA's stock dropped $600 billion in a single day because investors realized you don't need $100M training runs to get frontier AI.

What you get: Best price-to-quality ratio on the market. DeepSeek V3.1 costs $0.15 per million input tokens (vs $5 for GPT-5.5). R1 is a reasoning model competitive with OpenAI's o1 at ~30× lower cost. MIT-licensed — fully open.

What you give up: Direct API routes through China — many US/EU enterprises avoid for compliance reasons. Workaround: self-host the open weights or use a Western provider (Together, Fireworks) hosting the same models.

Use DeepSeek when: cost matters, you need open weights, or you're building high-volume products where margins depend on token economics.

Overview

Spun out of High-Flyer hedge fund. Released V3 (671B MoE) and R1 (reasoning) in late 2024 / early 2025 — both rivaling GPT-4 / o1 at a fraction of training cost (~$6M for V3).

Flagship models

  • DeepSeek V3.1 — general MoE
  • DeepSeek R1 — open reasoning model rivaling o1
  • DeepSeek-Coder V2 — code specialist

Why it matters

R1's release triggered a $600B NVIDIA single-day market cap drop. All models are MIT-licensed.

Architecture & scale

  • DeepSeek V3: 671B total / 37B active params (MoE)
  • DeepSeek R1: Reasoning model trained with pure RL (no SFT bootstrap)
  • Training cost V3: ~$5.6M reported (vs. $100M+ typical frontier)
  • Hardware: H800 (export-restricted variant); efficient MoE routing
  • MIT License — fully open weights and code

Benchmarks (May 2026)

  • MMLU-Pro: ~75% (V3.1)
  • GPQA Diamond: ~71% (R1)
  • MATH-500: ~97% (R1)
  • SWE-bench: ~49% (V3 + agent harness)
  • Matches or beats GPT-4o at ~1/30th the cost

Adoption

  • 2nd most-downloaded model family globally after Llama
  • Default cheap-tier in many production stacks
  • Available on Together, Fireworks, OpenRouter, Replicate, Hyperbolic
  • Banned/restricted on US federal devices (Mar 2025)
  • Hugely popular with Chinese enterprises and Indian/SEA startups

Background

  • Spun out of High-Flyer, a Chinese quantitative hedge fund
  • Funded entirely from High-Flyer profits (no VC)
  • ~150 engineers, very small team relative to impact
  • Hangzhou, China — operates outside Big Tech orbit

Industry impact

  • R1 release (Jan 2025) caused $600B NVIDIA single-day drop
  • Forced OpenAI, Anthropic, Google to slash prices in Q1-Q2 2025
  • Proved frontier capability doesn't require $100M+ training runs
  • Validated MoE architecture and aggressive distillation

Risks & considerations

  • Data privacy: Direct API routes through China — many enterprises avoid
  • Solution: self-host or use US-based providers (Together, Fireworks)
  • US export controls limit access to latest GPUs
  • Geopolitical risk — possible future restrictions
  • Some content policies aligned with Chinese regulation

Leadership

  • Liang Wenfeng — Founder & CEO (also runs High-Flyer)
  • Notably refuses VC, prefers independence
